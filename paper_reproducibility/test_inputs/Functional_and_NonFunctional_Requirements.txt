Functional Requirements
------------------------
FR1. The system shall detect the type of object (SODA or WATER) using distance sensors (D1 and D2).
FR2. The gripper shall OPEN and CLOSE according to the object type detected.
FR3. The robot shall grasp the product once it is detected.
FR4. The robot shall move from BANDi to HOMEi after grasping the object.
FR5. The robot shall place SODA in REDi and WATER in WHITEi.
FR6. The robot shall move back to BANDi via HOMEi after delivering the object.
FR7. The robot controller shall determine state transitions (INIT, BAND, HOME, RED, WHITE, MOVING).
FR8. The system shall ensure robots do not collide by satisfying safety constraints RQ1–RQ5.

Non-Functional Requirements
----------------------------
NFR1. Safety: The robot must not move to REDi or WHITEi when another robot is in or moving to the same area.
NFR2. Real-Time Responsiveness: Sensor readings and state transitions should be updated within the control timestep.
NFR3. Reliability: The system must consistently detect and classify products correctly under normal operating conditions.
NFR4. Modularity: Components such as gripper, robot controller, product detector, and conveyor must be independently modelled.
NFR5. Verifiability: The system must be verifiable using formal verification techniques such as timed automata and model checking.
NFR6. Scalability: The architecture should allow extension to more robots with similar interaction protocols.
NFR7. Maintainability: Software components should follow structured design with clear state transitions and modular architecture.
NFR8. Determinism: The behavior of gripper and robot controller should produce predictable outcomes under the same inputs.
